# Deltarune Lore: Roaring Knight, Dark Fountains, Titans, and "The Roaring" (state as of 2026-10-31)

> **Spoiler note:** These notes include plot points from Chapters 1 through 5. Each fact is tagged with the chapter it comes from, as [Ch. N]. Chapter 5 was released on June 24, 2026, so these notes go one chapter past the "Chapters 1–4" assumption in the brief.
>
> **How the sourcing works:** Most game-text claims come from deltarune.wiki, a community wiki whose articles cite in-game dialogue line by line. I read those pages through a summarising fetch tool, so the exact wording should be checked against the live page before anything is quoted. The Fandom wiki (deltarune.fandom.com) returned HTTP 402 and could not be used for a cross-check. I did not read the original deltarune.com newsletter text. Release facts come from games press and from the wiki's summary of the newsletter. Dialogue is paraphrased throughout, and these notes contain no direct dialogue quotes.
>
> **Legend:** Each question has the same subsections. **Cited Findings** separates (a) what the game or official material establishes from (b) fan interpretation. **Inferences** contains our own reasoning and our design takeaways, labelled as such. **Gaps** contains (c) open or unresolved questions plus anything the research could not verify.

## Q1. Which chapters are released as of October 2026, and what did Chapter 5 change for these topics?

Five chapters are out. Chapters 1–4 shipped as a paid bundle on June 5, 2025, and Chapter 5 shipped on June 24, 2026 as a free update. Chapter 5 adds new Dark Fountains, a new Knight attack that ends in Asgore's abduction, and hints that the Knight made fountains before the story began. It adds no new Titan or "Roaring" lore, and reviewers describe it as a lower-stakes chapter that holds back on lore.

## Q2. What are Dark Fountains: creation, sealing, consequences, and visual appearance?

### Takeaway
A Dark Fountain is a gushing pillar of darkness that turns a real-world place into a Dark World. Any determined Lightner can make one by driving a sharp object into the ground. Only Kris, through the SOUL, can seal one, which restores the original place. If too many are left open, the balance of Light and Dark breaks and the apocalypse called the Roaring follows. If one grows too strong, a Titan can hatch from it.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Established by game text (as cited by the wiki)**
- **Definition:** large, gushing pillars of darkness. Each one creates a localised Dark World layered over a Light World space, and the Darkners in it reflect the fountain's "will" (Ralsei's explanation) [Ch. 2]. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)
- **Creation:** Queen says any Lightner could create one by channelling determination into a sharp object and piercing the ground [Ch. 2]. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)
- **Confirmed creators:** the Knight made several (Library [Ch. 2], Dark Sanctuary and 3rd Sanctuary [Ch. 4]). Kris made the Dreemurr-residence fountain with a knife [Ch. 2] and the Flower Kingdom fountain [Ch. 5]. Susie made the 2nd Sanctuary fountain to open a door [Ch. 4]. The creators of the Grand Fountain and the Eastern Fountain [Ch. 1] and of the Shelter Fountain [end of Ch. 3] are unknown. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)
- **Kris's creation scene [Ch. 2]:** while Toriel and Susie sleep, Kris removes the SOUL, turns on the TV, and uses a knife to make a fountain. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)
- Queen wanted to use Noelle to make new fountains [Ch. 2]. — [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)
- King calls the Knight his own and credits it with pulling fountains up from the earth (paraphrase) [Ch. 1]. — [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)
- **Sealing:** the heroes seal fountains to restore the balance of Light and Dark. Ralsei says only Kris can seal one directly, because of the SOUL [Ch. 1]. Sealing reverts the Dark World to the Light World place it was based on. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)
- In Chapter 4, a phone Caller implies Kris cannot enter a Dark World without the SOUL. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)
- A seal does not always end a Dark World. After Kris sealed the Flower Kingdom fountain, the Dark World persisted because a second fountain had appeared [Ch. 5]. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain); [deltarune.wiki: Chapter 5](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Chapter%205)
- **Consequences if left unsealed:** Ralsei warns that each new fountain shifts the balance between Light and Dark. If that harmony breaks, the catastrophe called the Roaring follows [Ch. 1, repeated in Ch. 2]. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)
- If a fountain becomes too strong or releases too much darkness, a Titan can emerge from it [Ch. 2 warning, Ch. 4 events]. In Chapter 4, a fountain in the 3rd Sanctuary hardened into stone and "hatched" a Titan. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain); [deltarune.wiki: Titan](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Titan)
- **Visual appearance (as described by the wiki):**
  - Tall, gushing pillars of darkness. The Grand Fountain towers over Castle Town [Ch. 1].
  - When one is created in the Light World, it first shows as an opaque black fog that fills the location.
  - The church's Dark Sanctuary fountain is much thinner than the others [Ch. 4].
  - The Shelter Fountain is described as floating out of the shelter [end of Ch. 3].
  - Fountain scenes use a recoloured stock ocean image as the background (internal asset name `IMAGE_DEPTH`).
  - Source: [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)
- The prophecy places a Dark Fountain at the centre of the kingdom, with a second one appearing on the horizon [Ch. 1]. — [deltarune.wiki: Prophecy](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Prophecy)

**(b) Fan interpretation**
- I found no reliably sourced fan theory specifically about fountain mechanics in this session. Fan discussion centres on who made the unattributed fountains (Grand, Eastern, Shelter, pre-game). The pre-game fountain's creator is attributed to the Knight only through Susie's suspicion in Chapter 5. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)

### Inferences
- **Lore structure (our reading):** the canonical cycle is *pierce → darkness gushes up → a pocket world forms → the hero seals it → the place is restored*. Overflow causes two escalating failures: locally, one fountain grows too strong and hatches a Titan; globally, too many fountains stay open and the Roaring follows. This maps closely onto memory: allocation, then the working set growing, then one runaway process, then system-wide pressure. **Design takeaway (our interpretation):**
  - A "fountain forming at launch" would be an original upward-rising column of dark particles or fog that settles into the main gauge.
  - A "seal" would be the per-action verb for returning memory to the OS, since in the lore sealing means restoring a space to its original state.
- **Visual vocabulary to borrow (our interpretation):** vertical, rising, gushing motion. Fog that fills a space before it resolves into a form. Pillar thickness can carry magnitude, since the lore itself has a notably thin fountain, so thickness could encode a process's memory share.
- **What not to copy (our interpretation, not legal advice):** do not reproduce the specific ocean-image background or any sprite or tile art. Build original gradients and particles.

### Gaps
- **Colours beyond "black" or "dark", and specific motion such as shimmer or rainbow edges, sky colour, sound and the sealing flash:** the pages I read do not describe these. Anything more precise would come from memory or screenshots, which I did not verify.
- **What happens to Darkners when a fountain is sealed:** not stated on the pages I read.
- **The Shelter Fountain:** the Dark Fountain page lists it as revealed at the end of Chapter 3 and not shown sealed as of Chapter 5. The Roaring Knight page's end-of-Chapter-3 section does not mention a fountain in the shelter. This is a minor inconsistency between wiki pages.
- **The second Chapter 5 fountain:** the Dark Fountain page attributes it to the Knight. The Roaring Knight page says it does not state who created it. Its authorship is unresolved in my sources.

## Q3. Who or what is the Roaring Knight? Actions, broad visual qualities, and canon versus theory on identity

### Takeaway
The Knight is the main antagonist across the chapters and the boss of Chapter 3. It is a tall, black, white-outlined, shape-shifting silhouette that makes Dark Fountains, abducts adults from the main cast (Undyne in Ch. 3, Asgore in Ch. 5), and in Chapter 4 caused the first Titan. Its identity, motives and relationship to Kris are unrevealed as of Chapter 5. Every identity claim, whether Lightner, Carol, Dess, Rudy or Kris, is either a character's guess or a fan theory.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Established by game text (as cited by the wiki)**
- **Role:** main antagonist, and the final and Shadow Crystal boss of Chapter 3. It creates Dark Fountains and is linked to the prophecy and to the Roaring [Ch. 1–5]. The wiki uses it/its pronouns and notes they/them is also used. — [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)
- **Chapter 1 (mentioned, not seen):** Seam, King and the Former Kings describe its arrival and the chaos that followed. King treats it as his ally. Jevil foreshadows what is coming, alluding to a coming "roar" the Lightners must stop. Seam mentions a "strange son" in connection with the Knight's rise, without explaining it. — [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight); [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)
- **Chapter 2 (mentioned, not seen):** it created the Library fountain the day after Chapter 1, shown in a video at Queen's mansion. Spamton hints at a connection, then cuts himself off. — [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)
- **Chapter 3:**
  - Tenna says the Knight told him to keep the party busy in exchange for viewers.
  - After Tenna gives Toriel back, the Knight strikes him from behind and severs both his arms.
  - It tries to abduct Toriel, then fights the party. Its "defeat" ends with Susie and Ralsei knocked out and Kris kneeling at swordpoint.
  - Undyne interrupts a second abduction attempt with spears, so the Knight takes Undyne instead, through the Great Door.
  - Susie chases a figure toward the shelter, but the doors shut before she reaches it.
  - Source: [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)
- **Chapter 4:**
  - It makes two fountains at the church after the service (Dark Sanctuary and 3rd Sanctuary).
  - It attacks Kris and Susie in the Atrium, and Kris falls from a great height.
  - It repeatedly blocks the party. The Old Man says it flew through a locked Study door.
  - At the end, cornered, it mocks Susie's belief that it is powerless in the Light World. It then makes a fountain inside the Dark World, which hatches a Titan. It is not seen again in Chapter 4.
  - Source: [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)
- **Chapter 5:** it captures Asgore and leaves the Dark World with him, after fighting Flowery. Documents suggest it made fountains long before the party met it. — [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight); [deltarune.wiki: Chapter 5](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Chapter%205)
  - **Conflict:** the Roaring Knight page says the Knight "fatally wounds" Flowery and implies a death. The Chapter 5 page describes only a struggle followed by the Knight escaping with Asgore.
- **Broad visual qualities (descriptors only):**
  - A tall, slender, angular, pitch-black silhouette with a white outline.
  - A diamond-shaped helmet with antler-like protrusions, described as having a "frog-mouth" look.
  - White holes through the palms that emit a mauve, starry beam, which can pull objects toward it.
  - It levitates and leaves afterimages.
  - It can curl into a ball, take a fast-flying bird-like form, and distort when damaged.
  - When "roaring", it shows a monstrous face and a large eye in its chest.
  - It glows while charging, and its hands glow a strange colour before its full-screen "Roaring" attack.
  - Its battle attacks include stars, tracking swords, knife-shaped projectiles, and a sword-tunnel attack.
  - Source: [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)
- **Never unmasked:** in Chapter 4, Kris imagines it taking off its helmet, and the scene cuts to a gag instead. — [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)
- **The prophecy:** Chapter 4 glass-tapestry panels foretell a Knight who will duel the heroes. — [deltarune.wiki: Prophecy](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Prophecy)
- **Identity status in game text:** the wiki lists its relationship to Kris as "unknown" and its motives and origin as unknown. Susie's Chapter 4 belief that it is a Lightner is a guess, based on a figure she saw in the dark in the Light World. The wiki says the game never states that the Knight is a Lightner or that it will personally cause the Roaring. The name only references the prophesied event. — [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)

**(b) Fan interpretation (not canon)**
- **Dess Holiday** (Noelle's missing sister) is one of the most popular theories. Fan blogs argue the Chapter 5 reveals make it much less likely, because the Knight appears connected to Dess's disappearance. — [deltarune.wiki user blog: New Knight Information](https://deltarune.wiki/w/User:TheFlamingX/blogs/New_Knight_Information)
- **Carol Holiday** (Noelle's mother) and **Rudy Holiday** (Noelle's father) are also theorised. One fan blog argues for Rudy, and others argue against both. — [deltarune.wiki user blog: Rudy Knighting](https://deltarune.wiki/w/User:TheFlamingX/blogs/Rudy_Knighting); [deltarune.wiki user blog: New Knight Information](https://deltarune.wiki/w/User:TheFlamingX/blogs/New_Knight_Information)
- **Lightner versus Darkner:** a Steam forum post argues the Knight cannot be an ordinary Darkner. It appears in every sanctuary's Dark World, opens fountains, and exists in the Light World, which Darkners cannot do. — [Steam Community discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1671210/discussions/0/724650931393829381/)
- **Meme culture:** the fandom has an established meme around the Knight and a "black knife". This is fan culture, not lore. — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/the-roaring-knight-black-knife)
- "Kris is the Knight" circulates as a theory because Kris canonically makes fountains. I found it only on low-quality aggregator pages and did not cite those pages as evidence.

### Inferences
- **Our reading:** the Knight and Kris appear on screen together in Chapters 3 and 4, where the Knight attacks Kris. That makes a simple "Kris is the Knight" theory hard to square with the shown events, though Deltarune often subverts expectations.
- **Design takeaway (our interpretation):**
  - In the lore, the Knight's blade-stroke *opens* darkness, creating fountains. The heroes' SOUL is what *closes* it.
  - So a "knightly blade mark" for a deliberate user action fits tonally as a decisive, intentional cut. Lore-literate users may still read a blade as "opening" rather than "cleaning".
  - Consider pairing a blade-cut motion (the decisive gesture) with a closing or sealing effect (the result), so the metaphor runs from cut to seal.
- **Broad palette cues (our interpretation):** near-black forms, crisp white edge lines, and one restrained cool-violet accent echo the mood (silhouette, outline, starry beam) without copying. Avoid reproducing the exact silhouette: the diamond helmet with antler protrusions, palm holes, and chest eye are the character's distinctive likeness markers.
- Other motifs to borrow abstractly: afterimages and levitation (motion trails on a slow, deliberate animation), stars, and sudden shape changes (a compact form expanding into a fast form).

### Gaps
- **The Knight's identity, motive, relationship to Kris, and the meaning of Seam's "strange son" remark:** all unresolved as of Chapter 5.
- **Whether the Knight personally intends to bring about the Roaring:** not stated in game text.
- **Whether the Knight directed Tenna in person or through intermediaries** [Ch. 3]: unclear, per the wiki.
- **Whether Flowery dies in Chapter 5:** conflicting summaries.
- **Exact accent colours beyond "mauve":** not verified.
- **Reputable press coverage of identity theories:** not found in this session, and the Fandom wiki was inaccessible (HTTP 402).

## Q4. What is "The Roaring"? Prophecy and end-of-world event

### Takeaway
The Roaring is the prophesied apocalypse that follows when too many Dark Fountains exist at once and the Light–Dark balance breaks. Titans rise from the fountains and ravage the land, Darkners are crushed by the darkness and slowly turn to stone, Lightners are left in endless darkness, and the sky fills with fountains. It has not happened in the story as of Chapter 5. It is a warning and a prophecy, not a shown event.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Established by game text (as cited by the wiki)**
- **Definition:** an apocalyptic event in the prophecy, caused by an overabundance of Dark Fountains that plunges the world into chaos. Ralsei explains it near the end of Chapter 2, both in a full verse and in a shorter summary on the Weird Route. The summary says that if too much darkness is released, Titans emerge from the fountain and destroy everything. — [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)
- **Earlier foreshadowing:** Jevil alludes to it after being defeated by FIGHTing, warning of a hellish roar the Lightners must stop [Ch. 1]. — [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)
- **What happens, according to the prophecy:**
  - Titans spawn from Dark Fountains and devastate the land.
  - Darkners are crushed by the darkness and gradually turn to stone.
  - Lightners are left to fend for themselves in endless darkness.
  - The sky is filled with fountains.
  - Source: [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)
- **Broader prophecy framing [Ch. 1]:**
  - Light and Dark have long been balanced. If the balance breaks, the sky darkens, the land cracks, and the Earth nearly dies.
  - Three heroes appear (a Human, a Monster, and a Prince from the Dark). Only they can seal the fountains and banish the "Angel's heaven".
  - Source: [deltarune.wiki: Prophecy](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Prophecy)
- **Chapter 4 additions:** glass tapestries in the Dark Sanctuaries, a hymn sung by a choir, and a church reading. Freed fountains bring the Roaring, Titans rise from darkened eyes, and a Knight duels the heroes. A "Last Prophecy" hints that the worlds can be saved only at great cost. — [deltarune.wiki: Prophecy](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Prophecy)
- A Dark Sanctuary inscription also describes the Roaring [Ch. 4]. — [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)
- **Character attitudes:** Queen says she did not know what more fountains would cause. King believes more fountains consuming the world would let Darkners rule. The wiki does not give a chapter for these lines, but King's is likely Ch. 1 and Queen's Ch. 2. — [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)
- **Presentation:** Ralsei's Chapter 1 prophecy telling uses sepia-toned "legend" cutscenes, which also open the main menu for Chapters 1–3. Chapter 4 uses glass tapestries displayed as text panels with images. The wiki calls the prophecy accurate in fine detail but cryptic, and notes that Ralsei admits his version is a summary. — [deltarune.wiki: Prophecy](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Prophecy)
- The Knight's full-screen phase-4 battle attack is named "Roaring" [Ch. 3]. The wiki treats its link to the apocalypse as uncertain. — [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight); [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)

**(b) Fan and wiki-editor speculation (flagged as speculative on the wiki itself)**
- The game-over voice may imply the Roaring happens because no one is left to seal the fountains. — [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)
- The Titans flanking the central one in Ralsei's Roaring illustration resemble the Knight, which may hint at a connection. — [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring); [deltarune.wiki: Titan](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Titan)
- The central Titan's eye markings match engravings found in several locations, and their meaning is unexplained. — [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)

### Inferences
- **Important naming caution (our interpretation):** in canon, "The Roaring" is the *catastrophe*, the end state of unchecked darkness. It is not the cleanup. Using "The Roaring" as the name of R.R.'s system-wide cleanup *inverts* the lore. Lore-aware users may read the button as "trigger the apocalypse". Two options:
  - **(1)** Lean into the inversion on purpose, as a playful framing where the app "roars" to push darkness back.
  - **(2)** Reserve "Roaring" for the *danger state*, such as a critical memory-pressure banner ("the Roaring approaches"), and name the cleanup with sealing language such as "Seal All" or "Grand Seal". Option 2 is more lore-faithful and still uses the app's "Roaring" brand.
- **Design takeaway (our interpretation):**
  - The "balance" between Light and Dark fits as a framing for memory: a balance gauge (free versus used) rather than a bare usage bar.
  - The Roaring's imagery (darkening sky, cracking land, many fountains filling the sky, things slowly turning to stone) suggests a restrained escalation for the pressure states. Background dims, hairline cracks appear, multiple thin columns rise, and inactive or suspended items grey out to a "stone" state.
- The prophecy's "cryptic but accurate" quality fits a trend forecast, for example "at this rate, pressure reaches critical in about N minutes", presented as a prophecy-style panel. Use original framing such as a stained-glass-inspired card, not the game's sepia or glass art.

### Gaps
- **Whether the Roaring will actually happen or can be prevented, and what the "Last Prophecy" means:** unresolved as of Chapter 5.
- **The Angel and "Angel's heaven", and how they relate to the Roaring:** cryptic and unresolved. The Angel panel is not shown to the player, per the wiki.
- **Exact chapter attribution for the Queen and King lines and the Dark Sanctuary inscription:** not given on the page I read.

## Q5. What are Titans: chapters, formation, relation to darkness and fountains, and broad visuals?

### Takeaway
Titans are gargantuan, mindless, destroy-only beings the prophecy says will ravage both worlds during the Roaring. They are first warned of in Chapter 2. The only one fought so far is Chapter 4's final boss, which "hatched" from a fountain the Knight made inside a Dark World. They come from a fountain whose power grows too strong. They are black and white, cannot be spared, and were beaten by sealing the fountain from inside and striking with the SOUL. No new Titan appears in Chapter 5.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Established by game text (as cited by the wiki)**
- **Definition:** gargantuan creatures that the prophecy says will ravage the Light and Dark Worlds during the Roaring. The wiki calls them a major antagonistic force. — [deltarune.wiki: Titan](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Titan)
- **Chapters:**
  - **Chapter 2:** a warning only. On the Weird Route, Ralsei says Titans emerge from the fountain if too much darkness is released.
  - **Chapter 4:** a Titan is created and fought as the final boss, and Ralsei's explanation of the Roaring depicts three Titans.
  - The wiki lists Queen's Mansion (Ch. 2) as the first place Titans are depicted.
  - Sources: [deltarune.wiki: Titan](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Titan); [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)
- **Formation:**
  - Titans arise when a Dark Fountain's power becomes too strong [Ch. 2, Ch. 4].
  - In Chapter 4, the Knight made a fountain *inside* a Dark World, in the 3rd Sanctuary. That fountain hardened into stone and hatched a Titan. Ralsei says the "hatching" is happening very fast.
  - Sources: [deltarune.wiki: Titan](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Titan); [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)
- **Nature, per Ralsei [Ch. 4]:** Titans have no consciousness, exist only to destroy, and cannot understand Mercy. Ralsei compares them to the mind's imagined fear of the dark, much as Dark Worlds reflect imagination. The wiki treats this as a metaphor rather than a mechanism. — [deltarune.wiki: Titan](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Titan)
- **Prophecy panels [Ch. 4]:** Titans rise from darkened eyes. — [deltarune.wiki: Prophecy](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Prophecy)
- **Broad visuals [Ch. 4]:**
  - Enormous, with very high HP and defence.
  - Entirely black and white.
  - Emerges from a tall pillar.
  - In Ralsei's Roaring illustration, three Titans appear, and the two side ones resemble the Knight.
  - Source: [deltarune.wiki: Titan](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Titan)
- **Defeat [Ch. 4]:** the party seals the Titan's fountain from inside, which lets Kris vanquish it with the SOUL. — [deltarune.wiki: Titan](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Titan). The Dark Fountain page describes the order differently: Kris purifies the Titan, then seals the fountain. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)

**(b) Wiki-editor inference (marked "appears" on the wiki)**
- Titans spawn smaller "Titan Spawn" and reabsorb them to restore HP. They remain part of the fountain they came from, and sealing that fountain is what makes them defeatable. — [deltarune.wiki: Titan](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Titan)

### Inferences
- **Design takeaway (our interpretation):** the Titan lore maps onto "unusually large memory pressure" in several ways.
  - **Spawned by a source grown too strong:** a single process whose working set has outgrown its normal size.
  - **Hatches very fast:** sudden memory spikes.
  - **Cannot be reasoned with (no Mercy):** a process that will not shrink on a polite trim. It needs a decisive action or a user decision.
  - **Spawn reabsorbed to restore HP:** pages faulting back in right after a trim, which is the classic working-set "regrowth".
  - **Defeated by sealing its source from inside:** treat the root cause, not the symptom.
  - This supports a "Titan" badge for a process whose memory share crosses a high threshold, and a "regrowth" indicator after trimming.
- **Visual cue (our interpretation):** strictly black and white, enormous scale, and emerging from a pillar. A Titan state could be a monochrome, oversized silhouette or bar rising out of a process's column. Use no eye motifs copied from the game. If eyes are used at all, keep them generic and original.

### Gaps
- **The full Titan backstory** (origin of the "darkened eyes" iconography, why the side Titans resemble the Knight, and whether Titans relate to Darkners turning to stone): unresolved.
- **Whether more Titans appear in Chapter 5:** the Chapter 5 summary I read mentions none. I did not do a complete Chapter 5 check.
- **Visual detail of the Chapter 4 Titan boss beyond "black and white, enormous, emerges from a pillar":** not described on the pages I read. I did not fetch the boss's own wiki page.

## Q6. Themes and moods that can become restrained, original UI metaphors (design takeaways: our interpretation)

### Takeaway
The released chapters supply a coherent, reusable set of *mechanics* that are not tied to specific art: balance between light and dark, a decisive piercing act that opens darkness, sealing that restores, overflow that hatches something mindless, and a cryptic but accurate prophecy. These can drive R.R.'s metaphors and naming without reproducing characters, dialogue, music or assets. The one major lore mismatch is using "The Roaring" for a cleanup, since in canon it is the apocalypse.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Lore motifs that are established (sources above)**
- **Balance of Light and Dark,** which fountains disturb and sealing restores [Ch. 1–2]. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)
- **Determination plus a sharp object piercing the ground creates a fountain** [Ch. 2]. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)
- **Sealing reverts a space to its original state.** Only the SOUL-bearer can seal [Ch. 1]. — [deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)
- **Overflow escalates:** one fountain growing too strong hatches a Titan, and too many fountains bring the Roaring [Ch. 2, Ch. 4]. — [deltarune.wiki: Titan](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Titan); [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)
- **Imagery of the end:** the sky darkens and fills with fountains, the land cracks, and Darkners slowly petrify [Ch. 1–2, Ch. 4]. — [deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring); [deltarune.wiki: Prophecy](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Prophecy)
- **Prophecy as cryptic but accurate,** presented through sepia legend scenes [Ch. 1] and glass tapestries [Ch. 4]. — [deltarune.wiki: Prophecy](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Prophecy)
- **The Knight's broad look:** black silhouette, white outline, afterimages, levitation, and a mauve starry beam [Ch. 3–5]. — [deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)
- **Chapter 5's tone:** grief, loneliness, and an ending that is quiet rather than triumphant. — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/deltarune-chapter-5-the-festival-the-kotaku-review-2000710566)

**(b) Fan culture context**
- The Knight is a major subject of fan theory and memes, so lore-aware users will recognise "Roaring" and "Knight" references immediately. — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/the-roaring-knight-black-knife); [deltarune.wiki user blog: New Knight Information](https://deltarune.wiki/w/User:TheFlamingX/blogs/New_Knight_Information)

### Inferences
All of the following is **our interpretation and proposals, not lore.**

**Metaphor mapping:**

| Lore element | R.R. concept |
|---|---|
| Fountain forming | App launch or first scan: an original rising column of dark fog that settles into the main gauge |
| Pillar thickness | Per-process memory share |
| Seal / Sealing | Per-process or per-group trim. Sealing in canon restores the original space, which matches returning memory to the system |
| Blade mark / cut | Confirmation flourish on deliberate, user-initiated actions, such as a single clean diagonal stroke that resolves into a closing seal (cut, then seal) |
| Titan | A process above a high memory-share threshold, or a rapid spike. A "hatching" animation for sudden growth. A "regrowth" tag when memory re-faults after a trim |
| Stone / petrified | Suspended, idle or unresponsive processes shown greyed and desaturated |
| The Roaring | Preferably the name of the critical-pressure state or warning. If it must be the system-wide cleanup, frame it as "answering" or "quelling" the Roaring, or accept the inversion knowingly |
| Prophecy | Trend forecast panel, such as "pressure will reach critical in about N minutes", in an original stained-glass-inspired or engraved-panel style |
| Balance | A Light/Dark balance gauge (free versus committed) instead of a plain percentage bar |

**Mood and palette direction:** near-black surfaces, hairline white outlines, one cool-violet accent used sparingly for "power" moments, slow deliberate motion with subtle afterimage trails, vertical rising motion for growth, and inward collapsing motion for sealing. Keep it quiet and solemn. Chapter 5's reception suggests the series' strongest moments are understated.

**IP hygiene (our interpretation, not legal advice):**
- Avoid the Delta Rune emblem shape, the Knight's distinctive helmet and silhouette, palm-hole and chest-eye details, sprite or tile art, the ocean-image fountain background, the music and sound, and any dialogue.
- Treat "Roaring Knight" as a character name and do not use it in the UI.
- Generic words like "fountain", "seal", "titan", "prophecy" and "roar" are fine as metaphors.

### Gaps
- I did not check trademark or IP status of the names "Deltarune" or "Roaring Knight", or of the app name "Roaring Ram". That is outside this lore scope and would need separate review.
- I did not check fan sentiment on whether lore-inspired utilities are well received.
