# Roaring Ram Should Seal, Not Roar

As of October 31, 2026, Deltarune has five released chapters (Chapter 5 arrived as a free update on June 24, 2026), and together they firmly establish how the game's darkness *works* while leaving every *identity* open: Dark Fountains are made by driving a sharp object into the ground with determination, only Kris can seal one through the SOUL, a single fountain grown too strong hatches a mindless Titan (one was fought in Chapter 4), and too many open fountains bring the Roaring, a prophesied apocalypse that has not happened yet. Who the Roaring Knight is, what lies inside the Shelter, who pulled Spamton's strings, whether the framing Voice is W.D. Gaster, and what the grinning FRIEND is all remain unconfirmed, and every popular answer is fan theory. The most consequential design finding is a naming trap: in canon "the Roaring" is the catastrophe, so a cleanup button bearing that name reads to lore-literate users as "start the apocalypse," and the lore-faithful scheme reserves "Roaring" for critical memory pressure while naming actions with sealing language. On Windows, a non-elevated app can monitor almost everything, including system totals, commit, and per-process counters for every process, but it can act only on processes that run as the same user, are not elevated, and are not protected. Its soft requests (working-set trims and memory priority) are hints whose effects rebound; its one real limit, a job-object commit cap, makes new allocations fail, binds the process until it exits, and can crash it; and its only lever that works on any app is asking that app to close. Purging the standby list needs administrator rights and does not even lower "In use," and no API at any privilege level makes an arbitrary app use less memory. The lore and the platform point the same way: R.R. should promise accurate visibility and honest sealing of the user's own apps, not "freed RAM." *Spoilers through Chapter 5 follow.*

## Five chapters fix how darkness works but leave every identity open

Deltarune's canon now spans five chapters. Chapters 3 and 4 came out together in early June 2025 in a $24.99 package that also bundled the formerly free Chapters 1–2; the wiki dates the release to June 4, while press coverage of the Nintendo Switch 2 launch gives June 5 ([deltarune.wiki: Chapter 4](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Chapter_4); [RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/news/17139-deltraune-chapters-1-4-release-date-nintendo-switch-2-launch-title-ps5-ps4-steam)). Toby Fox promised buyers future chapters at no extra cost ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/toby-fox-says-youll-only-need-to-buy-deltarune-once-future-chapters-will-be-added-for-free/)), and **Chapter 5 shipped as a free update on June 24, 2026**, simultaneously on all platforms, two weeks after its reveal in the June 9 Nintendo Direct ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/nintendo-direct/2026/06/09/deltarune-chapter-5-launches-later-this-month); [deltarune.wiki: Chapter 5](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Chapter_5)). Sources do not even agree on its subtitle, which the wiki gives as "Festival Day" and Kotaku's headline as "The Festival" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/deltarune-chapter-5-the-festival-the-kotaku-review-2000710566)). Critics describe Chapter 5 as a quieter, character-driven chapter about grief and loneliness that deliberately avoids Chapter 4's big lore reveals ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/deltarune-chapter-5-the-festival-the-kotaku-review-2000710566); [Final Weapon](https://finalweapon.net/2026/06/26/deltarune-chapter-5-review/)). The practical consequence is that Titans and the Roaring still rest almost entirely on Chapters 1, 2 and 4. The wiki lists Chapter 6 as "planned 2027" and Chapter 7 as undated ([deltarune.wiki: Chapter 5](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Chapter_5)); that plan is unverified, but it means two chapters remain that could reframe anything built on an unresolved reveal.

### Fountains, Titans and the Roaring form one escalation ladder

A Dark Fountain is a tall, gushing pillar of darkness that lays a localized Dark World over a real place, populated by Darkners who, as Ralsei explains in Chapter 2, reflect the fountain's "will" ([deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)). Queen establishes that **any Lightner can make a fountain by channeling determination into a sharp object and piercing the ground**, and the game bears this out across its cast. The Knight made the Library fountain in Chapter 2 and two fountains at the church in Chapter 4; Kris removed the SOUL and made the Dreemurr-house fountain with a knife in Chapter 2, then made the Flower Kingdom fountain in Chapter 5; and Susie opened the 2nd Sanctuary fountain to get through a door in Chapter 4. Nobody knows who made Chapter 1's Grand and Eastern fountains or the Shelter fountain revealed at the end of Chapter 3 (same source). Sealing is the counter-move: **only Kris can seal a fountain directly, because of the SOUL**, and a seal reverts the Dark World to the ordinary place beneath it. Chapter 5 adds a wrinkle that matters for metaphor. Kris sealed the Flower Kingdom fountain, but the Dark World persisted because a second fountain had opened, which gave the Knight the opening to abduct Asgore before the party sealed that one too ([deltarune.wiki: Chapter 5](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Chapter_5)). Asgore's notes on "black shards," together with an earlier "Unknown Fountain" that Susie suspects the Knight made, push the Knight's fountain-making back before the story began ([deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)). Visually, the wiki describes tall gushing columns preceded in the Light World by an opaque black fog, a church fountain notably thinner than the rest, and fountain scenes backed by a recolored stock ocean image ([deltarune.wiki: Dark Fountain](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Dark_Fountain)).

Excess darkness escalates in two directions. **Locally, a single fountain that grows too strong can hatch a Titan.** Ralsei warns of this on Chapter 2's Weird Route, and in Chapter 4 the Knight made a fountain *inside* a Dark World that hardened into stone and hatched one at alarming speed ([deltarune.wiki: Titan](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Titan)). Titans are gargantuan, purely black and white, and emerge from a pillar; they have no consciousness, exist only to destroy, and cannot understand Mercy, and Ralsei likens them to the mind's imagined fear of the dark. The party beat the only Titan fought so far by sealing its fountain from inside so that Kris could strike it with the SOUL, although the Dark Fountain page reverses the order and puts purification before the seal. The wiki's claim that Titans release smaller "Titan Spawn" and reabsorb them to heal is editors' inference, which the page itself hedges with the word "appears" (same source). **Globally, too many open fountains break the balance of Light and Dark and bring the Roaring**, the prophesied apocalypse in which Titans rise from the fountains, Darkners are crushed and slowly turn to stone, Lightners are left in endless darkness, and the sky fills with fountains ([deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)). Jevil foreshadows it in Chapter 1, Ralsei explains it at the end of Chapter 2, and Chapter 4 extends the prophecy through glass tapestries, a choir hymn, and a "Last Prophecy" hinting that the worlds can be saved only at great cost ([deltarune.wiki: Prophecy](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Prophecy)).

**The Roaring has not happened as of Chapter 5**; it is a warning, not a shown event. Whether it can be averted, what the Angel's heaven is, and whether the Knight's full-screen Chapter 3 attack (also named "Roaring") connects to the apocalypse all remain open. The wiki's further suggestions, that the Game Over voice implies the Roaring follows when no one is left to seal, and that the side Titans in Ralsei's illustration resemble the Knight, are flagged as speculation on the page itself ([deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)).

### The Roaring Knight acts constantly on screen yet stays unidentified

The Knight is the series' main antagonist and Chapter 3's final and Shadow Crystal boss ([deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)). In Chapters 1–2 it is only mentioned: King calls it his own and credits it with pulling fountains up from the earth, and Spamton hints at a connection before cutting himself off. From Chapter 3 it acts on screen. It got Tenna to keep the party busy in exchange for viewers, then struck him from behind and severed his arms, defeated the party, and carried Undyne off through the Great Door. In Chapter 4 it opened two fountains at the church, sent Kris falling in the Atrium, flew through a locked study door, mocked Susie's belief that it is powerless in the Light World, and made the fountain that hatched the Titan. In Chapter 5 it fought Flowery and left with Asgore, although the wiki contradicts itself here as well: the Knight page says it fatally wounds Flowery, while the Chapter 5 summary describes only a struggle ([deltarune.wiki: Chapter 5](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Chapter_5)). In broad terms it reads as a tall, slender, pitch-black silhouette with a white outline, a diamond-shaped helmet with antler-like protrusions, and white holes in its palms that fire a mauve, starry beam. It levitates and leaves afterimages, shape-shifts into a ball or a fast bird-like form, and, when roaring, shows a monstrous face and an eye in its chest ([deltarune.wiki: Roaring Knight](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Roaring_Knight)).

What the game does not say is who it is. The wiki lists its origin, motives and relationship to Kris as unknown, notes that Susie's Chapter 4 belief that it is a Lightner is a guess based on a figure she glimpsed in the dark, and stresses that the game never states the Knight will personally cause the Roaring; the name only references the prophecy (same source). Chapter 4 even teases an unmasking and cuts to a gag. Every identity claim is therefore fan theory. Noelle's missing sister Dess Holiday is the most popular candidate, though fan bloggers argue that Chapter 5's reveals tie the Knight to Dess's disappearance and so weaken that reading, while her mother Carol and father Rudy each have advocates and critics ([deltarune.wiki user blog: New Knight Information](https://deltarune.wiki/w/User:TheFlamingX/blogs/New_Knight_Information); [deltarune.wiki user blog: Rudy Knighting](https://deltarune.wiki/w/User:TheFlamingX/blogs/Rudy_Knighting)). A Steam forum argument holds that the Knight cannot be an ordinary Darkner because it moves through every sanctuary's Dark World and the Light World ([Steam Community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1671210/discussions/0/724650931393829381/)). "Kris is the Knight" circulates because Kris canonically makes fountains, but it is hard to square with Chapters 3 and 4, where the two share the screen as attacker and victim. The fandom's "black knife" meme shows how instantly lore-literate users will recognize Knight imagery ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/the-roaring-knight-black-knife)).

## The Shelter, Spamton, Gaster and FRIEND are hinted at, never explained

### The Shelter waits behind a three-field lock with one code still missing

The shelter is a locked steel double door at the south edge of Hometown, south of the church and Town Hall. Kris can reach it from Chapter 1, where leaving the area triggers a brief audio glitch ([deltarune.wiki: Shelter](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Shelter)). Chapter 2 adds only rumors and a kick from Susie. Chapter 3 makes it central: a Shelter Key and a replica of the shelter appear inside the hidden "Original Game," and at the chapter's end the Knight carries Undyne inside, black smoke pours out, the door shuts, and Susie's pounding reveals **a keypad with three fields marked by a pine tree, a police badge and the Delta Rune**. The chapter closes with the door opening in front of Kris ([deltarune.wiki: Chapter 3](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Chapter_3)). In Chapter 4, Alphys says the shelter has been shut for as long as she has lived in town and that only Undyne and Carol go near it, and Kris finds the first code's opening digits, 122, inside Dess's guitar before Carol takes the guitar away. In Chapter 5, Flowery reveals that Asgore, a former police chief, hid his code in the golden flower's soil, and Kris digs up the full code 0915199X (199X0915 in the Japanese version). The Shelter page says Susie grabs the paper before Kris can destroy it, while the Chapter 5 summary says only that she interrupts ([deltarune.wiki: Shelter](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Shelter); [deltarune.wiki: Chapter 5](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Chapter_5)). The music near the door is `mus_smile`, the track from Undertale's hidden room, slowed to 15% speed, the same treatment heard in Noelle's basement in Chapter 4 ([deltarune.wiki: Shelter](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Shelter); [Undertale Wiki: Gaster](https://undertale.wiki/w/Gaster)).

Everything beyond that is interpretation, and the wiki flags all of it as speculation. It reads 0915199X as Undertale's September 15 release date joined to EarthBound's "199X" setting, connects the roughly 6.6-fold stretch of the 15% slowdown to a recurring six motif associated with Gaster, notes that the official survey gives one shared reply to answers mentioning the shelter, "Depths" or "Hell," and floats a Toby Fox interview mention of Porter Robinson's "Shelter" video as a possible allusion ([deltarune.wiki: Shelter](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Shelter)). The open questions are substantial: what the shelter's Dark World is, who made it and why it existed before Kris began making fountains, who holds the third code, and why the Knight can enter. A theory that Gaster gave the Knight the code surfaced in search but could not be traced to a stable source.

### Spamton's strings lead to an unnamed hand, and Gaster stays offscreen

Spamton is a Chapter 2 "Addison," a spam-mail salesman who became a big shot only after he started calling an unseen helper on the phone. Jealous Addisons cut him off, the helper vanished, his sales collapsed, and he ended up living in a Cyber City dumpster ([deltarune.wiki: Spamton](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Spamton)). During his fall he prayed in the Queen's Mansion basement before an old machine holding a crystal, a Lightner's abandoned dream project that Swatch helped build. An optional path through KeyGen, the EmptyDisk and the LoadedDisk turns that machine into Spamton NEO, a puppet hung on green wires whose attacks are built on phone calls and against whom the player's SOUL turns yellow and fires charged shots ([deltarune.wiki: Spamton NEO](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Spamton_NEO)). On the normal route the party snaps his wires, and he speaks plainly for the first time, calling himself only ever a puppet and hoping they can cut their own strings; on the Weird Route he cannot be spared and Noelle freezes him (same source). Until NEO is beaten, his speech is spam email made audible, full of bracketed ad copy, random capitals, number-for-letter swaps, abrupt cutoffs and a recurring censor tag ([deltarune.wiki: Spamton](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Spamton)). Later chapters add only traces. The Chapter 3 Original Game backstory shows Tenna partnering with him and getting him to sign a contract that he abandoned mid-signing to take a call, an Organikk echoes his censor phrase in Chapter 4, and in Chapter 5 the ReviveSong spell can summon an angelic Spamton (same source). The game never names the phone helper. The idea that W.D. Gaster pulled Spamton's strings, and Jevil's, is press and fan speculation that Wikipedia carries with a citation to the Washington Post and a flag for possible original research ([Wikipedia: W. D. Gaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._D._Gaster)), and the theory that Tenna's contract drove the helper away is the wiki's own speculation.

**Gaster has still not appeared by name or in person** through Chapter 5 ([Wikipedia: W. D. Gaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._D._Gaster)). The game instead offers a dense web of allusions it never confirms. Lines in Chapters 1, 3, 4 and 5 echo the escalating-darkness wording of Undertale's "Entry Number Seventeen"; Gaster's hidden-room sound returns as Dark World phone static and as the slowed shelter music; naming the vessel or player "GASTER" resets the intro exactly as in Undertale; the intro theme "ANOTHER HIM" carries his leitmotif; and repeated 666 values plus an installer versioned 0.6.6.6 sit in the code ([Undertale Wiki: Gaster](https://undertale.wiki/w/Gaster); [deltarune.wiki: W.D. Gaster](https://deltarune.wiki/w/W.D._Gaster); [deltarune.wiki: Voice](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Voice)). Framing the whole game is an unnamed **Voice** that speaks in polite, glowing, slow-scrolling capitals. It builds a "vessel" from the player's survey answers in Chapter 1 and then throws it away, offers a retry at every Game Over, returns after a Chapter 3 loss to the Knight, congratulates the player on reaching the halfway point after Chapter 4's credits, and has no unique scene in Chapter 5. Before each release, the official X and Bluesky accounts go blank-named and post in its style ([deltarune.wiki: Voice](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Voice)). Fans and much of the games press equate the Voice with Gaster, and some argue that the voice which discards the vessel differs from the survey Voice, but the game confirms neither. According to the Undertale Wiki, Toby Fox's only official public mention of Gaster outside a game is an oblique reference in Undertale's 2017 patch notes ([Undertale Wiki: Gaster](https://undertale.wiki/w/Gaster)), and he has said that only in-game content counts as canon ([deltarune.wiki: Spamton Sweepstakes](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Spamton_Sweepstakes)).

### "Friend" almost certainly means FRIEND, a grinning intruder that subtracts

The strongest referent for "Friend" is **FRIEND**, the wiki's all-caps name for a recurring, unexplained entity drawn as a black cat or bare face with a pink left eye, a yellow right eye and a huge crooked grin ([deltarune.wiki: FRIEND](https://deltarune.wiki/w/FRIEND)). It flashes in the dark Queen's Mansion basement where the NEO machine sits in Chapter 2. In Chapter 3 it laughs during Ralsei's explanation of Dark Worlds and appears as an enemy in the same Original Game dungeon that holds the shelter replica. In Chapter 4 it hides in the 3rd Sanctuary's dark room and drains SOUL fuel in the Battat minigame. In Chapter 5 it deals damage equal to the sell price of the first inventory item without ever causing a Game Over, prompts Pink to remark on a cat bite on Kris's hand, and surfaces as the word pair IMAGE/FRIEND in the final Flowery battle (same source). Its shared assets are the closest thing to evidence of a wider connection: its colors match the default Microsoft Paint palette, its laugh extends the Spamton Sweepstakes "face" sound, it shares a laugh sample with the Knight, and Spamton's glasses and NEO's wings carry its pink-and-yellow scheme (same source; [deltarune.wiki: Spamton NEO](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Spamton_NEO)). Readings tying it to the Cheshire Cat, to Undertale's Endogeny, or to foreshadowing on the Sweepstakes site are all speculation. Two weaker candidates exist: the Voice, which never calls itself a friend but repeatedly says it wants a connection with the player, and Undertale's "friend" motif, now echoed by Chapter 5's Flowery, a plant born from the golden flower who befriends Asgore ([deltarune.wiki: Chapter 5](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Chapter_5)). If any part of R.R.'s design hinges on which "Friend" was meant, the user should confirm it.

Across all four, the register is the same. Text misbehaves through glowing capitals, bracketed censorship and mangled grammar; familiar sounds return slowed almost to stillness; a presence is implied and never shown; and things are held in place by something offscreen, whether strings, a lock split among families, or a discarded vessel. Monochrome dominates, broken by one or two accent hues.

## Lore-faithful metaphors make the Roaring a warning, not a button

The single largest risk in R.R.'s theming is its own name. In canon, **the Roaring is the catastrophe**, the end state of unchecked darkness, not the cleanup that prevents it ([deltarune.wiki: The Roaring](https://deltarune.wiki/w/The_Roaring)). A system-wide "Roaring" cleanup button therefore inverts the lore, and lore-literate users will read it as "start the apocalypse." Two coherent fixes exist. R.R. can embrace the inversion as a knowing joke in which the app roars back at the darkness, or, more faithfully, it can reserve "Roaring" for the danger state, a critical-pressure banner announcing that the Roaring approaches, and name its actions with sealing language such as "Seal" and "Grand Seal." The second option keeps the brand while aligning it with a canon in which heroes seal and the Roaring is what happens when they fail. The same inversion lurks in blade imagery: the Knight's cuts *open* fountains, so a blade flourish alone reads as making darkness, while pairing one decisive cut with a closing seal turns the gesture into cut-then-close.

The Windows research below sharpens the sealing metaphor in a way the lore already anticipates. In canon, a seal shuts off the source and restores the place, yet Chapter 5 shows a seal failing to end a Dark World while a second fountain stays open. A working-set trim behaves like that failed seal, because pages leave the working set but stay in RAM, fault straight back when touched, and leave commit untouched. A job-object commit cap behaves like a true seal, because it shuts off the source by refusing new commits. The honest naming therefore gives "Seal" to the OS-enforced cap and a softer verb, such as "push back" or "quiet," to trims, paired with a visible regrowth tag that also mirrors the wiki's description of Titans reabsorbing their spawn. The Titan, which cannot be spared, fits a process that no API can persuade to shrink: the only options are to cap it or to close it.

The protective line is to borrow mechanics and moods, never likenesses. Generic words such as fountain, seal, titan, prophecy and roar work as metaphors, but R.R. should keep out of its UI the character names (Roaring Knight, Spamton, Gaster, FRIEND), the Delta Rune emblem, the Knight's helmet-and-antler silhouette with its palm holes and chest eye, all music and sound including any resampling of the slowed track, all dialogue, Spamton's "big shot" phrase, Kromer currency and censor tag, Wingdings and 666 numerology, the shelter's glyphs and code digits, and the pink-and-yellow pair. That list is a cautious design judgment rather than legal advice, and nobody has yet checked trademark status for "Roaring Ram" or "Deltarune." Two constraints are unique to a RAM tool. The lore's intrusions are adversarial, since FRIEND drains and resets and Spamton upsells, so any glitch flavor in R.R. must look visibly decorative, never touch numbers that carry meaning such as megabytes freed or PIDs, respect reduced-motion settings, and never imitate a salesman's pop-ups. And only Chapter 1 material (the vessel intro, the locked door, Ralsei's first telling of the prophecy) is spoiler-safe for every player, so copy that alludes to Chapter 3–5 events such as the abductions belongs outside the default UI.

## A standard-user app can see every process but touch only the user's own unelevated apps

Monitoring is where a non-elevated app is strongest, and three documented system-wide calls need no process handle at all. **GlobalMemoryStatusEx** fills a MEMORYSTATUSEX structure whose `dwMemoryLoad` gives an approximate 0–100 percentage of physical memory in use and whose `ullAvailPhys` is defined as the standby, free and zero lists combined, which means **reusable file cache already counts as available** ([MEMORYSTATUSEX](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/sysinfoapi/ns-sysinfoapi-memorystatusex)). Its `ullTotalPageFile` is a trap: it reports the commit limit of the system *or of the calling process, whichever is smaller*, so if a terminal, IDE or launcher started R.R. inside a job, the number describes R.R.'s own clamp rather than the machine. **GetPerformanceInfo** supplies the true system-wide commit picture (CommitTotal, CommitLimit and CommitPeak) along with SystemCache, which is the standby list plus the system working set, and the paged and nonpaged kernel pools. Every memory field is expressed in pages that must be multiplied by PageSize, and CommitLimit is itself soft while the page file can grow ([PERFORMANCE_INFORMATION](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/psapi/ns-psapi-performance_information)). **GetPhysicallyInstalledSystemMemory** returns installed RAM in kilobytes from SMBIOS firmware tables, a figure that legitimately exceeds the OS-visible total because firmware and drivers reserve memory for memory-mapped I/O ([GetPhysicallyInstalledSystemMemory](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/sysinfoapi/nf-sysinfoapi-getphysicallyinstalledsystemmemory)).

Matching Task Manager is approximate by necessity. Microsoft documents the mapping between Task Manager and these APIs only for the Vista-era tool ([Memory Performance Information](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/memory/memory-performance-information)), while the Windows 10/11 labels ("In use (Compressed)," "Cached," and "Committed" shown as used/limit) are described only by community sources ([HelpDeskGeek](https://helpdeskgeek.com/task-manager-ram-meaning-explained/); [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/319933/what-is-memory-compression-in-windows-10/)). The defensible formula is In use ≈ `ullTotalPhys − ullAvailPhys`, which includes the modified list and the compression store, labeled "approximately matches Task Manager," with committed memory drawn from GetPerformanceInfo. Real pressure shows up as commit approaching its limit while available memory runs low, not as a high "In use," because Windows deliberately keeps freed memory as cache. Per-process numbers never add up to the system total either. A shared page sits in every working set that maps it, so summing WorkingSetSize double-counts ([Working Set](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/memory/working-set)), and the file cache, kernel pools and compressed memory are not attributed to ordinary processes in a summable way. The compression store originally lived in the System process's working set on Windows 10, and later builds report it under "In use (Compressed)" instead ([TweakHound mirror of the Windows Insider post](https://www.tweakhound.com/2015/08/20/windows-10-memory-compression/); [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/319933/what-is-memory-compression-in-windows-10/)). For a top-consumers list, private commit and private working set are the only metrics that can safely be summed.

Per-process data comes by two routes. The fast route is NtQuerySystemInformation with SystemProcessInformation (class 5), loaded dynamically from ntdll, where one call returns a consistent snapshot of every process, with image name, PID, working set, private page count and page-file usage, without opening a single handle ([NtQuerySystemInformation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winternl/nf-winternl-ntquerysysteminformation)). Community testing reports that for a standard user it covers every process except PID 0, while the SystemFullProcessInformation variant requires administrator rights ([psutil issue #304](https://github.com/giampaolo/psutil/issues/304); [TheWover gist](https://gist.github.com/TheWover/71079af504ba8e056c9ebbe017d288a0)). The costs are real: Microsoft warns that the function and its structures can change or disappear, the private working set sits in a field Microsoft labels only Reserved1 (Geoff Chappell's reverse-engineered layout identifies it as WorkingSetPrivateSize), and the two sources even disagree on whether PeakWorkingSetSize is measured in kilobytes or bytes ([Geoff Chappell: SYSTEM_PROCESS_INFORMATION](https://www.geoffchappell.com/studies/windows/km/ntoskrnl/api/ex/sysinfo/process.htm)). Two recent changes leave the route intact. Windows 11 24H2's withholding of kernel addresses from callers without SeDebugPrivilege targets pointer-bearing classes rather than memory counters ([windows-internals.com](https://windows-internals.com/kaslr-leaks-restriction/)), and build 26100.4770 added a lighter SystemBasicProcessInformation class whose SequenceNumber detects PID reuse but which carries no memory counters ([NtQuerySystemInformation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winternl/nf-winternl-ntquerysysteminformation)).

The documented route is OpenProcess with PROCESS_QUERY_LIMITED_INFORMATION, followed by K32GetProcessMemoryInfo from kernel32 and QueryFullProcessImageNameW for the path ([GetProcessMemoryInfo](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/psapi/nf-psapi-getprocessmemoryinfo); [QueryFullProcessImageNameW](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winbase/nf-winbase-queryfullprocessimagenamew)). Its richest structure, PROCESS_MEMORY_COUNTERS_EX2 with PrivateWorkingSetSize and SharedCommitUsage, exists only on Windows 10 22H2 or Windows 11 22H2 with the September 2023 cumulative update, so R.R. must feature-detect by passing the EX2 size and falling back on failure ([PROCESS_MEMORY_COUNTERS_EX2](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/psapi/ns-psapi-process_memory_counters_ex2)). For the koffi bindings, sizes computed from the documented x64 layouts are 64 bytes for MEMORYSTATUSEX, 104 for PERFORMANCE_INFORMATION, 72, 80 and 96 for the three process-counter structures, and 144 for JOBOBJECT_EXTENDED_LIMIT_INFORMATION; none has yet been confirmed with a sizeof test. Event-driven monitoring is also free of privilege. CreateMemoryResourceNotification returns a waitable handle for system-wide low- or high-memory conditions, with a deliberate middle band where neither fires ([CreateMemoryResourceNotification](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/memoryapi/nf-memoryapi-creatememoryresourcenotification)). A notify-only job limit (Windows 8 and later) watches a job's commit against a threshold while its processes keep allocating; its notifications are guaranteed to arrive, but the monitor must query the violation information before another is sent ([JOBOBJECT_NOTIFICATION_LIMIT_INFORMATION](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winnt/ns-winnt-jobobject_notification_limit_information); [Job Objects](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/procthread/job-objects)). The catch is that assigning a process to a job in order to watch it is itself permanent, as the next section explains.

Which processes R.R. can touch depends on each target's security, not on R.R.'s intentions. OpenProcess checks the requested rights against the target's access list ([Process Security and Access Rights](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/procthread/process-security-and-access-rights)); mandatory integrity labels stop a medium-integrity process from modifying higher-integrity ones ([Windows Integrity Mechanism Design](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/previous-versions/dotnet/articles/bb625963%28v=msdn.10%29)); and protected processes refuse PROCESS_QUERY_INFORMATION, PROCESS_SET_QUOTA, PROCESS_SET_INFORMATION and virtual-memory access to every other process while still allowing the limited query right ([Process Security and Access Rights](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/procthread/process-security-and-access-rights)).

| Target process | Read memory (QUERY_LIMITED) | Trim, working-set cap, or job (SET_QUOTA; plus TERMINATE for a job) | Memory priority (SET_INFORMATION) | WM_CLOSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Same user, medium integrity | Yes | Yes | Yes | Delivered |
| Same user, elevated | Usually yes (community-reported) | **No** (integrity) | **No** | Silently dropped |
| SYSTEM, service or other user | Typically no; counters still available via class 5 | **No** | **No** | Not applicable |
| Protected (PPL) | Yes, limited query | **No** | **No** | Not applicable |

Browser and Electron sandbox processes are an undocumented gap. They run with restricted tokens and custom access lists and often sit in jobs already, so R.R. should expect access-denied errors on them until testing proves otherwise.

## Trims rebound, caps bite, and purges need an administrator

**No Windows API makes another application use less memory.** Everything R.R. can do falls into one of four honesty classes, and the UI should label each action accordingly.

| Action | API and access right | Class | Reachable without elevation | What actually happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Read system memory and commit | GlobalMemoryStatusEx, GetPerformanceInfo (no handle) | MONITOR | The whole system | Point-in-time totals. Commit limit stays soft while the page file can grow |
| Read per-process counters | NtQuerySystemInformation class 5 (no handle). K32GetProcessMemoryInfo (QUERY_LIMITED) | MONITOR | All processes via class 5. Processes whose access list allows it via PSAPI | A snapshot. Some class 5 fields are undocumented |
| Watch for pressure | CreateMemoryResourceNotification. Notify-only job limit | MONITOR | System-wide. Jobs R.R. owns | Signals only. The job route requires a permanent assignment |
| Trim working set | K32EmptyWorkingSet (QUERY_LIMITED + SET_QUOTA) | SOFT | Same-user, non-elevated, non-protected processes | Pages move to the standby or modified list, stay in RAM and fault back. Commit unchanged |
| Lower memory priority | SetProcessInformation with ProcessMemoryPriority (SET_INFORMATION) | SOFT | Same as above, Windows 8 and later | Changes trim order only under pressure |
| Hard working-set cap | SetProcessWorkingSetSizeEx with QUOTA_LIMITS_HARDWS_MAX_ENABLE (SET_QUOTA) | LIMIT | Same as above | Caps residency and forces paging. Frees no commit |
| Commit cap | Job object with JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_PROCESS_MEMORY or JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_JOB_MEMORY (SET_QUOTA + TERMINATE to assign) | LIMIT, often DISRUPTIVE | Same as above | New commits fail. Membership is permanent. Children join |
| Ask the app to close | PostMessage with WM_CLOSE | DISRUPTIVE | Windows at the same integrity level | The app can prompt, save, ignore or exit. Silently dropped for elevated targets |
| Kill the process | TerminateProcess (PROCESS_TERMINATE) | DISRUPTIVE | Same-user, non-elevated processes | Immediate exit. Unsaved data lost |
| Purge the standby list | NtSetSystemInformation class 80, command 4, with SeProfileSingleProcessPrivilege | Requires elevation | None | Discards cache already counted as available. "In use" does not change |

### Soft requests rebound the moment the app touches its memory

The soft requests are weaker than "memory optimizer" marketing implies. EmptyWorkingSet needs the query right plus PROCESS_SET_QUOTA, and Microsoft describes the operation as "useful primarily for testing and tuning" ([EmptyWorkingSet](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/psapi/nf-psapi-emptyworkingset); [Working Set Information](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/psapi/working-set-information)). Trimmed pages become transition pages that stay cached in RAM until they are referenced again, which costs only a soft fault, or until Windows repurposes them. Dirty pages must be written out before reuse ([Working Set](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/memory/working-set)), and on Windows 10 and later they are compressed rather than sent straight to the page file ([TweakHound](https://www.tweakhound.com/2015/08/20/windows-10-memory-compression/)). Commit never moves, "Available" rises only by the clean pages and only until the app touches them again, and R.R. should therefore call the action "trim working set (temporary)," never "free RAM." Memory priority is the most honest way to deprioritize an app. It takes a value from 1 (very low) to 5 (normal, the default), needs PROCESS_SET_INFORMATION on Windows 8 and later, only tells the memory manager what to trim first under pressure, and is undone by setting it back to 5 ([SetProcessInformation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/processthreadsapi/nf-processthreadsapi-setprocessinformation); [MEMORY_PRIORITY_INFORMATION](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/processthreadsapi/ns-processthreadsapi-memory_priority_information)).

### Job commit caps are the only control with teeth, and they never let go

The real limit is the job object, and it bites. R.R. creates a job, sets `JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_PROCESS_MEMORY` (0x100) for a per-process cap or `JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_JOB_MEMORY` (0x200) for a job-wide cap through the extended-limit class (class 9), and assigns the target using a handle that carries PROCESS_SET_QUOTA and PROCESS_TERMINATE ([AssignProcessToJobObject](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/jobapi2/nf-jobapi2-assignprocesstojobobject); [JOBOBJECT_BASIC_LIMIT_INFORMATION](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winnt/ns-winnt-jobobject_basic_limit_information)). From then on, **any commit beyond the cap fails**. The limit does not trim or kill; it refuses the allocation, and many programs treat that as fatal, as in Raymond Chen's account of a program that reported a fatal error because it ran inside a job with a 155 MB commit limit ([The Old New Thing](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/oldnewthing/20170419-00/?p=95995)). The cap is also **permanent**, because Microsoft states that a process can never leave the job it was assigned to, and the job persists while any member lives ([Job Objects](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/procthread/job-objects)). It does not look backward, since memory committed before assignment is not examined ([AssignProcessToJobObject](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/jobapi2/nf-jobapi2-assignprocesstojobobject)), and it spreads, since child processes join the job by default and, on Windows 8 and later, a process already in a job can join an empty job as a nested job and inherits the most restrictive limit in the chain. Setting KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE would make the last handle's closing terminate every member, so R.R. must not set it ([JOBOBJECT_BASIC_LIMIT_INFORMATION](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winnt/ns-winnt-jobobject_basic_limit_information)). The honest pattern is a named job in the `Local\` namespace, a handle kept open (or reopened by name) so the user can raise or remove the cap by querying, clearing the flag and setting the limits again, and a plain statement that the process stays in the job until it exits. A hard working-set maximum, set through `SetProcessWorkingSetSizeEx` with `QUOTA_LIMITS_HARDWS_MAX_ENABLE`, is the other limit. It caps residency, which forces the target to page against itself; it neither frees commit nor prevents allocation, and Microsoft warns that working-set changes affect the rest of the system ([SetProcessWorkingSetSizeEx](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/memoryapi/nf-memoryapi-setprocessworkingsetsizeex)).

### Closing is the only universal lever, and UIPI hides its failures

The only lever that works on any app at the same integrity level is asking it to close. WM_CLOSE lets the app prompt the user, save, or ignore the request ([WM_CLOSE](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/winmsg/wm-close)). When the target is elevated, however, User Interface Privilege Isolation makes PostMessage *return success while silently dropping the message* ([Windows Integrity Mechanism Design](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/previous-versions/dotnet/articles/bb625963%28v=msdn.10%29)), so R.R. must check the target's integrity level first and show "cannot close elevated apps from here" rather than trust the return value. TerminateProcess needs PROCESS_TERMINATE and loses unsaved data, which puts it behind a strong warning if R.R. offers it at all ([Process Security and Access Rights](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/procthread/process-security-and-access-rights)).

### Elevation unlocks a purge that changes nothing users watch

Elevation is required to purge the standby list, which uses the undocumented `NtSetSystemInformation` class 80 with the `MemoryPurgeStandbyList` command (4) and SeProfileSingleProcessPrivilege; a non-elevated caller receives STATUS_PRIVILEGE_NOT_HELD ([git test-drop-caches.c](https://code.googlesource.com/git/+/HEAD/t/helper/test-drop-caches.c); [bitshifter gist](https://gist.github.com/bitshifter/c87aa396446bbebeab29)). Even with elevation, the purge is pointless for the number users watch. Standby already counts as available, so purging leaves "In use" exactly where it was and throws away cache that would have saved disk reads later. Elevation is also required to act on the same user's elevated processes, to gain full access to other users' and SYSTEM processes (which additionally requires SeDebugPrivilege), and to use the SystemFullProcessInformation class.

### Six things no user-mode app can do at any privilege

Some limits hold for any user-mode app, elevated or not. No API forces an arbitrary app to shrink its commit; no call removes a process from a job; no cap reclaims commit made before it was applied; no trim lasts once the app touches its pages again; no process can change a protected process's memory settings, because PROCESS_SET_QUOTA and PROCESS_SET_INFORMATION are refused to every other process; and no medium-integrity app can deliver WM_CLOSE across UIPI. Reproducing the Windows 10/11 Task Manager "In use" figure exactly is also out of reach, because Microsoft has not published the formula.

### Linux kills where Windows refuses, and macOS offers no cap

For context, Linux cgroup v2 is the closest analogue but behaves differently. Its `memory.max` reclaims and then invokes the OOM killer, while `memory.high` throttles without ever killing ([Linux kernel: Control Group v2](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html)), and unprivileged users can apply these only inside a delegated subtree, which in practice means launching an app under a limit rather than capping one already running. On macOS, no documented API caps another app's memory, and `purge` requires sudo ([Pplware](https://pplware.sapo.pt/?p=549640)). Windows is unusual in refusing the allocation rather than killing the process.

### Five behaviors still need testing on real machines

Several behaviors could not be confirmed from sources and need empirical tests before R.R. ships: whether SystemProcessInformation really covers every process on 22H2, 24H2 and 25H2; which error the EX2 structure returns on builds without the September 2023 update; whether `JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_WORKINGSET` needs a privilege and whether it is a hard limit; what happens when a commit cap is set below a process's current usage; and whether R.R. can assign sandboxed Chromium or Electron processes to a job at all.

## Conclusion

The lore's rules and Windows' rules rhyme closely enough that R.R.'s theme can teach rather than decorate. Only the SOUL-bearer can seal a fountain, and only a handle the OS grants PROCESS_SET_QUOTA can cap a process. A Titan cannot be spared, and no API can persuade an app to shrink; it can only be capped or closed. Chapter 5's seal failed while a second fountain stayed open, just as a trimmed working set regrows the moment the app touches its memory. The locked Shelter, visible but untouchable, is the SYSTEM, elevated and protected memory that R.R. can report on but never change. Metaphors built on these parallels explain the real limits instead of hiding them, which makes this a rare case where theming makes a utility *more* honest. It also means R.R.'s core product is the gauge, not the button: its most valuable features need no special rights at all, namely accurate commit-versus-limit pressure, process totals that can safely be added up, and a forecast that warns before the Roaring arrives.

Two kinds of shelf life follow. In the lore, Chapters 6 and 7 can reframe the Knight, the Titans and the Roaring, so feature names built on game mechanics (seal, fountain, pressure) will hold up while anything that leans on an identity theory will date the day a chapter ships. On the platform, the job-object cap is the only control with real force, and because a process can never leave a job, assigning one deserves the same gravity as killing a process: explicit consent, a plain warning that it lasts until the process exits, and a way to relax the cap afterwards.
